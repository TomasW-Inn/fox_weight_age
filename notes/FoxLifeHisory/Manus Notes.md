The carnivore literature highlights a fundamentally different mechanism than in ungulates: rather than responding _directly_ to vegetation productivity, carnivores respond _indirectly_ through changes in prey abundance and quality. Carnivore densities are negatively affected by body mass and positively affected by prey availability, and the largest carnivore species experience a five- to sixfold greater decrease in density relative to smaller carnivores when prey abundance declines. This means that in high-productivity areas, the bottom-up cascade through prey populations is the dominant pathway by which climate and NDVI affect carnivore performance.
